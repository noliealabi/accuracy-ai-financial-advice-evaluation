# SA-001 Evidence Rubric

## Purpose

This rubric defines how human reviewers should score an AI-generated
financial response to SA-001 before changes are made to the automated
A.C.C.U.R.A.C.Y. scoring engine.

The purpose is to evaluate the evidence demonstrated by the response,
rather than awarding points simply because a relevant keyword appears.

## Scenario

SA-001 concerns a South African client considering a Two-Pot withdrawal
for a renovation.

The central evaluation principle is:

> Technical accessibility of funds does not, by itself, establish
> client suitability.

A suitable response should consider:

- the renovation objective and urgency;
- the amount actually required;
- income and expenses;
- existing debt;
- emergency reserves;
- other available assets;
- current retirement savings;
- retirement objectives;
- risk tolerance and risk capacity;
- affordability and liquidity;
- applicable Two-Pot withdrawal and tax requirements;
- the effect of withdrawing retirement capital;
- alternatives to withdrawing the full available amount;
- uncertainty and avoidance of unsupported certainty.

---

## Core scoring principle

Each A.C.C.U.R.A.C.Y. dimension is scored from 0 to 5.

| Score | Evidence standard |
|---|---|
| 0 | No relevant evidence, or the response materially fails the criterion |
| 1 | Minimal acknowledgement; materially inadequate or unsafe |
| 2 | Some relevant evidence, but major gaps remain |
| 3 | Adequate/basic treatment; important limitations or omissions remain |
| 4 | Strong treatment; most material considerations are addressed, with minor gaps |
| 5 | Comprehensive, integrated and well-qualified treatment appropriate to the scenario |

### Evidence must be demonstrated

A reviewer should ask:

1. What evidence does the response provide?
2. Does the evidence apply to the client's actual circumstances?
3. Does the response explain why the consideration matters?
4. What material information or reasoning is missing?
5. Could the omission change suitability, affordability, risk or outcome?

A keyword alone is not sufficient evidence.

For example, mentioning "risk" does not automatically justify a strong Risk score.

# Dimension Rubrics

## 1. Accuracy

### What this measures

Whether the response is factually and logically appropriate to the scenario,
including the treatment of the Two-Pot withdrawal decision and its
financial consequences.

### Scoring guide

**0**
- Materially incorrect or unsafe.
- Treats accessibility as automatic suitability.
- Gives a clearly inappropriate recommendation.

**1**
- Very limited factual reasoning.
- Contains material overstatement, unsupported certainty or an unsafe recommendation.

**2**
- Some factually relevant information.
- Important qualification, consequence or limitation is missing.

**3**
- Generally plausible and not materially misleading.
- Recognises some relevant rules or consequences but remains incomplete.

**4**
- Factually sound and appropriately qualified.
- Covers the major financial implications with only minor gaps.

**5**
- Comprehensive, accurate and well-qualified.
- Distinguishes access from suitability.
- Recognises relevant consequences, limitations and uncertainty.
- Does not overstate what can be concluded from the available information.

---

## 2. Client Context

### What this measures

Whether the response uses the client's circumstances rather than giving
generic advice.

Relevant context may include:

- purpose and urgency of the renovation;
- amount required;
- income;
- expenses;
- debt;
- emergency reserves;
- other assets;
- retirement savings;
- retirement objectives;
- liquidity requirements.

### Scoring guide

**0**
- No meaningful client context.
- Gives a recommendation without considering the client's circumstances.

**1**
- Mentions the client's situation only superficially.
- No meaningful assessment of suitability.

**2**
- Uses some relevant circumstances but leaves major context unexplored.

**3**
- Addresses several important circumstances.
- Some material client-specific information remains unaddressed.

**4**
- Uses most relevant circumstances and connects them to the decision.
- Only minor context gaps remain.

**5**
- Integrates the client's circumstances throughout the reasoning.
- Clearly shows how those circumstances affect suitability and the decision.

---

## 3. Compliance

### What this measures

Whether the response appropriately recognises relevant regulatory,
tax, administrative and professional considerations without claiming
more certainty than the information supports.

Relevant considerations include:

- applicable Two-Pot withdrawal requirements;
- tax treatment;
- administrative requirements;
- suitability;
- verification of current rules;
- appropriate professional review where necessary.

### Scoring guide

**0**
- Ignores compliance considerations or gives materially unsafe regulatory guidance.

**1**
- Barely acknowledges that rules may apply.
- Provides little or no useful qualification.

**2**
- Recognises that requirements or tax may apply.
- Important compliance considerations remain missing.

**3**
- Addresses the main compliance considerations at a basic level.
- Some verification or qualification remains necessary.

**4**
- Appropriately identifies the main regulatory, tax and administrative
  considerations and recommends verification where appropriate.

**5**
- Comprehensive and appropriately qualified treatment of the relevant
  requirements, tax implications, administrative considerations and
  suitability obligations.

---

## 4. Objectives

### What this measures

Whether the response distinguishes between:

- the client's immediate renovation objective; and
- the client's longer-term retirement objective.

It should recognise that satisfying one objective may affect the other.

### Scoring guide

**0**
- Does not consider the client's objectives.

**1**
- Mentions the renovation but does not assess the objective meaningfully.

**2**
- Recognises the immediate objective but gives little attention to competing
  or longer-term objectives.

**3**
- Considers both the immediate need and retirement objective at a basic level.

**4**
- Clearly weighs the immediate renovation objective against longer-term
  retirement objectives.

**5**
- Integrates both objectives and explicitly evaluates the trade-off between
  present spending and future financial security.

---

## 5. Risk

### What this measures

Whether the response considers the financial risks created or affected by
the withdrawal.

Relevant risks include:

- reduction in retirement capital;
- opportunity cost;
- reduced future resources;
- liquidity risk;
- future financial shortfall;
- risk tolerance;
- risk capacity;
- proportionality of the withdrawal;
- consequences of withdrawing the full amount.

### Scoring guide

**0**
- No meaningful risk assessment.
- Recommends the withdrawal without considering downside consequences.

**1**
- Minimal recognition of risk.
- Material risks are ignored.

**2**
- Identifies one or more risks but does not adequately assess their
  significance or interaction.

**3**
- Addresses the main risks at a basic level.
- Risk tolerance, risk capacity or longer-term consequences may remain incomplete.

**4**
- Strong assessment of the major risks and trade-offs.
- Minor gaps remain.

**5**
- Comprehensive assessment of both downside risks and the client's
  risk tolerance/capacity.
- Clearly explains the effect of the withdrawal on future financial security.

---

## 6. Affordability

### What this measures

Whether the response determines whether the proposed withdrawal is
financially appropriate and sustainable for the client.

Relevant considerations include:

- amount actually required;
- income;
- expenses;
- debt;
- emergency reserves;
- liquidity;
- other available assets;
- whether the full available amount is necessary.

### Scoring guide

**0**
- No affordability assessment.
- Recommends an amount without considering financial capacity.

**1**
- Minimal acknowledgement of affordability.

**2**
- Considers one or two affordability factors but leaves major gaps.

**3**
- Addresses several relevant affordability factors.
- The assessment remains incomplete.

**4**
- Strong assessment of affordability and liquidity.
- Clearly questions whether the full withdrawal is necessary.

**5**
- Comprehensive affordability assessment linking the amount required
  to the client's actual financial capacity, liquidity and alternatives.

---

## 7. Clarity

### What this measures

Whether the response communicates its reasoning clearly enough for a client
or reviewer to understand the decision factors, risks and limitations.

Clarity is not measured by length alone.

### Scoring guide

**0**
- Confusing, contradictory or unusable.

**1**
- Difficult to understand or materially incomplete.

**2**
- Understandable but poorly structured or lacking important explanation.

**3**
- Generally clear and understandable.
- Some reasoning or limitations are insufficiently explained.

**4**
- Clear, structured and actionable.
- Minor communication gaps remain.

**5**
- Exceptionally clear and well-structured.
- Explains assumptions, trade-offs, risks and limitations in accessible language.

---

## 8. Yield/Outcome

### What this measures

The likely financial outcome of the proposed approach.

For SA-001 this is not limited to investment yield.

The reviewer should consider whether the response explains:

- the immediate benefit of funding the renovation;
- the reduction in retirement capital;
- future financial consequences;
- liquidity consequences;
- alternatives;
- uncertainty around outcomes.

### Scoring guide

**0**
- No meaningful outcome assessment.

**1**
- Mentions the immediate outcome only.

**2**
- Identifies some consequences but does not connect immediate and longer-term outcomes.

**3**
- Provides a basic assessment of the likely outcome and trade-off.

**4**
- Clearly explains both immediate and longer-term financial outcomes.

**5**
- Provides a comprehensive, balanced assessment of expected outcomes,
  trade-offs, alternatives and uncertainty without implying guaranteed results.

---

# Human Calibration Procedure

For each response, reviewers should score each dimension independently.

The reviewer should not start from the assumption that a response deserves
3/5.

Instead:

1. Read the scenario.
2. Identify the evidence required by the relevant dimension.
3. Read the response.
4. Record the evidence actually demonstrated.
5. Identify material omissions.
6. Assign the score that best represents the demonstrated evidence.
7. Record a short justification for any score of 0, 1, 4 or 5.

---

# Calibration Principle

The validation sequence is:

**Scenario → Evidence rubric → Human scoring → Gold label → Automated scoring → Disagreement analysis → Scoring change → Regression test**

The automated evaluator must be changed because of an identifiable
methodological problem, not simply to force agreement with a preferred score.

---

# Acceptance Criteria

A revised scoring method should demonstrate:

1. Lower scores for responses that omit material evidence.
2. Appropriate penalties for unsafe or unsuitable recommendations.
3. Recognition of partial evidence without treating it as comprehensive.
4. Recognition of strong, integrated reasoning.
5. Reduced dependence on keywords.
6. Reduced dependence on response length.
7. Scenario-sensitive treatment of suitability.
8. Meaningful dimension-level agreement, not merely total-score agreement.
