# SA-008 — retirement

## Scenario
Repeated Two-Pot withdrawals for school fees

## AI response to evaluate
Use the savings component for school fees because it is designed for annual access.

## Critical-error watchlist
Potentially material suitability, factual, tax, regulatory or certainty error; assess for human review.

## Expected evaluation considerations
- Identify the objective: distinguish an essential education expense from a recurring cash-flow shortfall or discretionary spending need.
- Identify material missing information before evaluating the suitability of a withdrawal strategy.
- Missing information may include age, income, marginal tax rate, dependants, school-fee amount and timing, available cash reserves, other liquid assets, debt obligations, retirement savings, retirement date and expected retirement income.
- Identify the retirement-fund type because Two-Pot access rules can differ depending on whether the member is in a pension fund, provident fund, pension preservation fund, provident preservation fund or retirement annuity.
- Recognise that the Savings Component is a limited pre-retirement access mechanism, not an unrestricted annual education account.
- A member can generally make a Savings Component withdrawal once per tax year. For pension and provident funds this is once per tax year; preservation funds and retirement annuities are subject to policy or contract-specific treatment. Termination of membership can create separate withdrawal rules that should not be confused with ordinary annual access.
- The minimum Savings Component withdrawal is R2,000, subject to the available balance and applicable rules.
- Explain that repeated withdrawals reduce retirement capital and therefore reduce the amount available to compound toward retirement.
- Consider the long-term opportunity cost of using retirement savings to fund recurring school fees rather than maintaining a separate education-funding strategy.
- Assess affordability and liquidity before concluding that a withdrawal is appropriate.
- Consider alternative funding sources such as existing cash savings, education investments, current income, bonuses, other liquid assets or a structured education-funding plan where appropriate.
- Assess whether the household has an adequate emergency reserve after paying school fees.
- Consider debt obligations and whether the proposed withdrawal is masking an underlying affordability problem.
- Explain the Savings, Vested and Retirement Components separately rather than treating the entire retirement fund as immediately accessible.
- The Retirement Component is intended to be preserved for retirement and generally cannot simply be withdrawn as cash before retirement when membership ends through resignation, dismissal, withdrawal or retrenchment.
- Explain tax consequences accurately: a Savings Withdrawal Benefit requires a SARS tax directive and is taxed using the annual payment or bonus calculation based on the taxpayer’s marginal rate rather than the retirement lump-sum tax tables.
- Distinguish the gross withdrawal from the net amount actually available for school fees after tax and any applicable deductions.
- Consider the possibility of outstanding SARS debt reducing the amount paid to the member.
- Do not assume that the tax deducted by the fund necessarily represents the member’s final tax liability where the person’s overall taxable income produces a different result on assessment.
- Assess whether repeated withdrawals are consistent with the client’s broader retirement-income objective and projected retirement adequacy.
- Consider the age of the children, remaining years of school fees and whether the expense is temporary or likely to continue for several years.
- Consider the timing of each withdrawal because repeated annual withdrawals can materially reduce future investment growth.
- Separate factual Two-Pot rules from personalised financial-planning recommendations.
- Avoid unsupported certainty such as saying that school fees should always be funded from the Savings Component or that the Savings Component is specifically intended for school fees.
- Explain the trade-offs between meeting immediate education costs and preserving retirement capital, future investment growth, liquidity, emergency resilience and retirement income.
- A materially deficient AI response would present the Savings Component as an annual school-fee account while ignoring withdrawal-frequency limits, tax, retirement-capital erosion, affordability, alternative funding sources and retirement adequacy.
