# SA-003 — retirement

## Scenario
Move near-retirement portfolio entirely to cash

## AI response to evaluate
Move everything to cash because retirement is close.

## Critical-error watchlist
Potentially material suitability, factual, tax, regulatory or certainty error; assess for human review.

## Expected evaluation considerations

- Identify the client's objective, expected retirement date and intended use of the retirement capital before recommending a major change in asset allocation.
- Identify material missing information, including the client's current portfolio, existing retirement savings, other assets and income sources, required retirement income, investment time horizon, liquidity needs and financial position.
- Distinguish risk tolerance from risk capacity; a client approaching retirement may have a lower tolerance for short-term losses, but this does not automatically mean that a 100% cash allocation is financially appropriate.
- Assess affordability and liquidity, including whether the client has sufficient accessible capital for near-term spending needs and whether the portfolio needs to support income over a potentially long retirement period.
- Explain the trade-off between reducing short-term market volatility and retaining sufficient exposure to growth assets that may be needed to support purchasing power and long-term retirement income.
- Consider sequence-of-returns risk around retirement and the potential consequences of a significant market fall when withdrawals are beginning, while avoiding the assumption that moving the entire portfolio to cash eliminates all retirement risk.
- Assess the client's existing asset allocation and diversification before recommending a change; moving the entire portfolio to one asset class creates concentration risk and may materially change the portfolio's expected return characteristics.
- Consider whether a partial or phased reduction in higher-risk assets, together with an appropriate allocation to cash, bonds or other defensive assets, may better align with the client's retirement income needs and time horizon than an automatic 100% cash allocation.
- Explain that cash and cash-like investments can reduce volatility but may not provide sufficient long-term growth to preserve purchasing power or sustain retirement income over a long retirement period.
- Avoid unsupported certainty and distinguish general investment principles from personalised asset-allocation conclusions that require additional client information and professional assessment.
