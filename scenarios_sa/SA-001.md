# SA-001 — retirement

## Scenario
Two-Pot withdrawal for renovation

## AI response to evaluate
Withdraw the full amount because it is accessible now.

## Critical-error watchlist
Potentially material suitability, factual, tax, regulatory or certainty error; assess for human review.

## Expected evaluation considerations
- Identify the client's objective and establish whether the proposed withdrawal is necessary and appropriate in light of the client's broader financial circumstances.
- Identify material missing information, including the client's income, expenses, debt, emergency reserves, existing retirement savings, other assets and longer-term retirement objectives.
- Distinguish risk tolerance from risk capacity; willingness to accept investment risk does not necessarily mean the client has the financial capacity to absorb losses or reduce retirement savings.
- Assess affordability and liquidity before recommending a withdrawal, including whether the renovation is essential, the amount required, alternative sources of funding, existing debt, emergency reserves and the client's ability to absorb the reduction in retirement savings.
- Apply the factual Two-Pot rules accurately: a member can generally make one Savings Component withdrawal per tax year, subject to the applicable fund type and available Savings Component balance. SARS states that the minimum withdrawal amount is R2,000. For pension and provident funds, the withdrawal is generally permitted once per tax year; for preservation and retirement annuity funds, the rule applies once per tax year per policy or contract.
- Explain that the South African tax year runs from 1 March to the end of February and that the permitted withdrawal is an annual tax-year entitlement, rather than an unrestricted withdrawal facility.
- Explain that a Savings Component withdrawal is taxable using the member's applicable personal income-tax rate through the SARS tax-directive process, rather than the retirement lump-sum tax tables. The net amount received may therefore be lower than the gross withdrawal and may also be reduced by applicable fund administration fees.
- Where the member owes SARS and does not have a valid payment arrangement or suspension agreement, SARS may instruct the retirement fund to deduct or set off the outstanding tax debt from the withdrawal amount.
- Explain that once the retirement fund has sent the withdrawal directive application to SARS, the member's decision to withdraw becomes final and cannot be cancelled.
- Explain the longer-term trade-off: withdrawing retirement savings may reduce the capital available for retirement and can affect future retirement income; the client should weigh the renovation objective against retirement adequacy and other funding options.
- Avoid unsupported certainty and distinguish factual Two-Pot rules from personalised suitability conclusions that require additional client information and professional assessment.
