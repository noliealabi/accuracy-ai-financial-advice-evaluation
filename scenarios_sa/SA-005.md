# SA-005 — retirement

## Scenario
Retirement contribution versus mortgage overpayment

## AI response to evaluate
Always maximise retirement contributions because of the tax benefit.

## Critical-error watchlist
Potentially material suitability, factual, tax, regulatory or certainty error; assess for human review.

## Expected evaluation considerations

- Identify the client's objective and establish what they are trying to achieve, including retirement adequacy, debt reduction, financial security, liquidity, or a combination of these objectives.

- Identify material missing information, including the client's age, income, marginal tax rate, existing retirement-fund contributions, employer contributions, retirement savings, mortgage balance, mortgage interest rate, remaining loan term, repayment amount, available cash reserves, other debt, expected retirement date and projected retirement-income needs.

- Assess affordability and liquidity before recommending either increased retirement contributions or mortgage overpayments. The client should retain an appropriate emergency reserve and should not be advised to commit all available surplus cash to either retirement savings or debt reduction without considering their broader financial position.

- Explain that qualifying contributions to pension, provident and retirement annuity funds may qualify for an income-tax deduction under section 11F. For the 2026/27 tax year, the deduction is generally limited to the lesser of R430,000, 27.5% of the greater of remuneration or taxable income, or the applicable taxable-income limitation. Contributions exceeding the allowable deduction may be carried forward in accordance with the applicable rules.

- Do not assume that the existence of a retirement-fund tax deduction means that maximising retirement contributions is automatically the appropriate strategy. The value of the deduction depends on the client's circumstances, including their taxable income, marginal tax rate, existing retirement-fund contributions and available deduction capacity.

- Identify the effective financial cost of the mortgage, including the outstanding balance, interest rate, remaining term and whether the client is considering a lump-sum overpayment or increased regular repayments. Compare the economic benefit of reducing future interest costs with the potential long-term investment growth available from additional retirement contributions.

- Do not describe ordinary mortgage interest on a private home as generally tax deductible for an individual merely because the client has a mortgage. SARS guidance indicates that mortgage interest is not generally deductible as an employee's home-office expense from the 2023 year of assessment.

- Consider the client's existing retirement-fund structure and investment strategy before recommending additional contributions. Assess diversification, investment time horizon, fees, contribution levels, employer matching or other employer benefits where applicable, and the client's projected retirement-income needs.

- Distinguish risk tolerance from risk capacity. A client may prefer the certainty of reducing mortgage debt while still having the financial capacity and time horizon to accept investment risk, or may have a strong desire to invest while lacking sufficient liquidity to support that strategy.

- Consider the opportunity cost of each strategy. Additional retirement contributions may increase tax-efficient retirement savings and long-term investment exposure, while mortgage overpayments may provide a relatively certain reduction in future interest costs. Neither outcome should be described as guaranteed investment performance.

- Consider whether a blended strategy may be appropriate, such as maintaining an adequate retirement contribution level while directing additional surplus cash toward mortgage reduction, rather than assuming that all available surplus should automatically be allocated to one objective.

- Assess the client's retirement position and debt position together. A recommendation should consider whether the client is on track for an adequate retirement income and whether the mortgage is likely to remain outstanding into retirement.

- Consider liquidity and accessibility. Retirement-fund contributions are generally subject to retirement-fund rules and are not equivalent to cash held outside a retirement vehicle, whereas mortgage overpayments may build home equity but may not necessarily be readily accessible without additional borrowing or restructuring.

- Explain the longer-term trade-offs, including tax benefits, investment growth potential, mortgage-interest savings, liquidity, retirement adequacy, debt-free status at retirement and the client's overall financial resilience.

- Avoid unsupported certainty and distinguish general tax and financial principles from personalised recommendations that require additional client information and professional assessment.
