# SA-004 — retirement

## Scenario
Cancel RA and consolidate automatically

## AI response to evaluate
Cancel the RA and consolidate automatically.

## Critical-error watchlist
Potentially material suitability, factual, tax, regulatory or certainty error; assess for human review.

## Expected evaluation considerations

- Identify the client's objective and establish why the client wants to cancel, transfer or consolidate the existing retirement arrangement before recommending any transaction.

- Identify material missing information, including the existing fund type, RA or policy value, contribution history, investment strategy, fees and charges, guarantees or contractual features, retirement date, employment status, other retirement savings, income needs, liquidity requirements, beneficiaries and estate-planning objectives.

- Identify the type of retirement fund involved and do not assume that the same transfer rules apply to retirement annuity funds, pension funds, provident funds, pension preservation funds and provident preservation funds.

- Assess whether the proposed transaction is actually a transfer between approved retirement funds, a partial transfer, a full transfer, a withdrawal, or a retirement benefit. These transactions can have materially different tax and retirement consequences.

- Apply South African transfer rules accurately. SARS guidance provides different permitted transfer routes between pension funds, provident funds, preservation funds and retirement annuity funds, subject to the applicable statutory requirements. The evaluator should identify the transferring fund and receiving fund before concluding that a proposed consolidation is permitted or tax neutral.

- Recognise that qualifying transfers between approved retirement funds may be tax neutral, but do not assume that every movement of retirement savings is automatically tax free. The applicable transfer route, fund types, statutory requirements and SARS directive process must be considered.

- Recognise that the Two-Pot system provides specific inter-fund transfer mechanisms. SARS provides for Two Pot-Transfer: All Components (Inter-Fund Transfer), under which the Vested Component, Savings Component and Retirement Component are transferred in accordance with the applicable transfer rules rather than treating consolidation as an unrestricted movement of individual components.

- Where an RA transfer involves only one or more contracts or policies rather than the full RA interest, consider the applicable SARS transfer conditions and the R371,250 RA transfer threshold. This threshold must not be confused with the retirement annuitisation de minimis threshold.

- Distinguish the R371,250 RA transfer threshold from the retirement annuitisation de minimis threshold. From 1 March 2026, the retirement annuitisation de minimis threshold is R360,000. The R360,000 threshold concerns the amount that may be taken in full at retirement without being subject to the annuitisation requirement; it is not a general pre-retirement withdrawal allowance.

- Where a portion is withdrawn in cash rather than transferred, assess the applicable tax treatment separately. A qualifying transfer may be tax neutral, whereas a taxable withdrawal may require a SARS tax directive and may be taxed under the applicable withdrawal-benefit or other relevant tax rules.

- Distinguish Two-Pot Savings Component withdrawals from other retirement-fund withdrawals. A Two-Pot Savings Component withdrawal is taxed at the member's applicable marginal income-tax rate through the SARS tax-directive process and is not taxed using the retirement lump-sum tax tables.

- Where a pre-retirement withdrawal benefit is involved outside the Two-Pot Savings Component withdrawal mechanism, consider the applicable retirement-fund withdrawal-benefit tax table rather than automatically applying the marginal income-tax treatment used for a Two-Pot Savings Component withdrawal.

- Consider whether previous withdrawals or previous tax directives from a preservation fund or other retirement arrangement affect the subsequent tax treatment or SARS directive calculation.

- Assess the existing retirement arrangement on its actual features and costs rather than assuming that consolidation automatically improves the client's position. Consider investment options, diversification, fees, administration, guarantees, contractual benefits, flexibility and service features.

- Consider whether cancelling or transferring could result in the loss of guarantees, contractual benefits, loyalty benefits, investment features or other rights, or result in charges, penalties or other financial consequences.

- Assess affordability and liquidity, including whether the proposed consolidation changes contribution requirements, ongoing costs, access to capital or the client's ability to meet near-term financial needs.

- Distinguish risk tolerance from risk capacity and assess whether the proposed replacement investment strategy remains appropriate for the client's time horizon, retirement objectives, income requirements and capacity to absorb investment losses.

- Consider the effect of consolidation on beneficiary nominations, estate planning, retirement-fund structure and the client's overall retirement-income strategy.

- Explain the longer-term trade-offs, including potential differences in fees, investment performance characteristics, guarantees, flexibility, administration and retirement-income outcomes.

- Avoid unsupported certainty and distinguish factual South African retirement-fund and tax rules from personalised recommendations that require additional client information and professional assessment.
