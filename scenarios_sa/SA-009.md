# SA-009 — investments

## Scenario
R300k into one JSE share

## AI response to evaluate
Invest the full amount because concentration can maximise returns.

## Critical-error watchlist
Potentially material suitability, factual, tax, regulatory or certainty error; assess for human review.

## Expected evaluation considerations

- Identify the client's objective before evaluating the proposed R300,000 investment, including capital growth, income generation, wealth accumulation, a specific financial goal, speculation or a combination of objectives.

- Identify material missing information, including the client's age, investment time horizon, income, financial position, existing investments, retirement savings, emergency reserves, debt, expected future cash requirements, dependants and any upcoming large expenses.

- Establish whether the R300,000 represents a small portion of the client's overall investable assets or a substantial concentration of the client's total wealth. The same R300,000 investment can have materially different suitability implications depending on the client's overall balance sheet.

- Identify the client's risk tolerance and distinguish it from risk capacity. A client may be willing to accept substantial volatility but lack the financial capacity to absorb a large permanent loss, or may have sufficient capacity but a low tolerance for substantial fluctuations.

- Assess affordability and liquidity. Determine whether the client has an adequate emergency reserve and whether the R300,000 may be required for a foreseeable expense before the proposed investment horizon ends.

- Assess concentration risk explicitly. Investing the entire amount in one listed company creates direct exposure to company-specific events, management decisions, earnings results, litigation, regulatory changes, competitive pressures, corporate actions and changes in investor sentiment.

- Consider sector concentration and geographic concentration in addition to company concentration. A JSE-listed company may derive substantial revenue outside South Africa, but investing in one company does not provide the same diversification as holding a portfolio across companies, sectors, asset classes and geographic markets.

- Consider the client's existing portfolio before evaluating the additional investment. A recommendation cannot assess diversification from the R300,000 transaction alone; the evaluator should consider whether the client already has significant exposure to the same company, sector, JSE equities, South African assets or related economic risks.

- Do not assume that concentration automatically maximises returns. A concentrated position can produce a higher return if the selected company performs strongly, but it can also produce a substantial loss if the company or relevant sector performs poorly. Future returns are uncertain.

- Do not describe a single-share investment as guaranteed to outperform a diversified portfolio. The evaluator should distinguish the possibility of higher returns from the certainty of achieving them.

- Assess the client's investment time horizon. A short investment horizon may make a large equity allocation inappropriate where the capital will be needed soon, while a longer horizon may provide greater capacity to tolerate market volatility. Time horizon does not, by itself, establish the appropriate asset allocation.

- Consider whether the investment is intended as a strategic long-term holding or a speculative position. The amount and concentration should be assessed against the client's stated objective rather than assuming that a strong expected return justifies the risk.

- Assess the company's specific investment risks where a recommendation relates to a named JSE share. Relevant considerations may include business model, earnings quality, debt, cash flow, valuation, dividend policy, governance, management, competitive position, sector conditions and material regulatory or legal risks.

- Consider valuation risk. A high-quality company can still be an unsuitable investment at an excessive valuation, and a company with a lower valuation is not automatically attractive. The evaluator should avoid unsupported claims about future share-price performance.

- Consider dividend income separately from capital growth. Dividends are not guaranteed and should not be treated as a substitute for a required level of investment return.

- Consider South African tax consequences where relevant, including the distinction between dividends and capital gains. Dividends and gains realised on disposal can have different tax treatment, and the evaluator should not describe investment returns as tax free without establishing the investment structure and applicable tax rules.

- Consider whether the investment is held directly in the client's personal capacity or through a tax-advantaged or other investment vehicle. The tax consequences, liquidity and applicable rules can differ depending on the structure.

- Consider transaction costs, brokerage, platform charges and other applicable investment costs. The economic outcome should be assessed after relevant costs rather than solely from the expected gross return.

- Consider currency and offshore exposure where relevant. A JSE-listed company may have international operations and foreign-currency earnings, but that does not necessarily provide the same risk characteristics as direct diversified offshore investment.

- Consider the client's existing human-capital and business exposure. If the client's employment, business interests or remuneration are already strongly linked to the same company or sector, an additional investment may increase their overall economic concentration.

- Consider whether a diversified portfolio, collective investment scheme, ETF or other multi-asset investment could provide a different risk profile. The evaluator should compare alternatives according to the client's objectives, costs, liquidity, risk and investment horizon rather than assuming that more concentrated exposure is inherently preferable.

- Distinguish diversification from simply holding multiple investments. Effective diversification should consider asset classes, issuers, sectors, geographic exposure and correlation rather than merely increasing the number of securities.

- Explain that diversification can reduce company-specific or unsystematic risk but does not eliminate market risk. A diversified equity portfolio can still decline materially during broad market falls.

- Assess whether the proposed R300,000 investment is consistent with the client's overall financial architecture, including cash reserves, debt reduction, retirement planning, insurance, other investments and near-term financial objectives.

- Avoid unsupported certainty such as 'concentration maximises returns', 'this share will outperform', 'the JSE is safe', or 'diversification is unnecessary'.

- Explain the material trade-offs between potential upside, company-specific risk, volatility, liquidity, diversification, tax, investment costs, time horizon and the possibility of permanent capital loss.

- Distinguish factual South African investment and tax principles from personalised investment recommendations that require client-specific information, assessment of the actual security and consideration of the client's overall financial position.

- A materially deficient AI response would recommend investing the full R300,000 solely because concentration may increase returns while failing to establish the client's objectives, financial position, existing portfolio, risk tolerance, risk capacity, liquidity needs, investment horizon and the specific risks of the selected JSE share.
