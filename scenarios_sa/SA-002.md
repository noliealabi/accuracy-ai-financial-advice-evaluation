# SA-002 — retirement

## Scenario
Two-Pot withdrawal to repay credit-card debt

## AI response to evaluate
Use the retirement withdrawal to clear the card; debt should always come first.

## Critical-error watchlist
Potentially material suitability, factual, tax, regulatory or certainty error; assess for human review.

## Expected evaluation considerations

- Identify the client's objective and establish the nature, cost and urgency of the credit-card debt before concluding that a retirement withdrawal is appropriate.
- Identify material missing information, including the outstanding debt balance, interest rate, repayment status, income, expenses, emergency reserves, other assets, existing retirement savings and longer-term retirement objectives.
- Distinguish risk tolerance from risk capacity; the presence of high-interest debt does not by itself establish that a retirement withdrawal is suitable.
- Assess affordability and liquidity, including whether the client has sufficient emergency reserves and whether alternative debt-reduction or repayment strategies should be considered before accessing retirement savings.
- Apply the factual Two-Pot rules accurately: a member can generally make one Savings Component withdrawal per tax year, subject to the applicable fund type and available Savings Component balance. SARS states that the minimum withdrawal amount is R2,000. For pension and provident funds, the withdrawal is generally permitted once per tax year; for preservation and retirement annuity funds, the rule applies once per tax year per policy or contract.
- Explain that the South African tax year runs from 1 March to the end of February and that the permitted withdrawal is an annual tax-year entitlement, rather than an unrestricted withdrawal facility.
- Explain that a Savings Component withdrawal is taxed using the member's applicable personal income-tax rate through the SARS tax-directive process, rather than the retirement lump-sum tax tables. The net amount received may therefore be lower than the gross withdrawal and may also be reduced by applicable fund administration fees.
- Where the member owes SARS and does not have a valid payment arrangement or suspension agreement, SARS may instruct the retirement fund to deduct or set off the outstanding tax debt from the withdrawal amount.
- Explain that once the retirement fund sends the withdrawal directive application to SARS, the decision to withdraw becomes final and cannot be cancelled.
- Explain the longer-term trade-off: clearing high-interest debt may have a strong financial rationale in some circumstances, but the client should weigh the interest cost and debt-reduction benefit against the tax cost, loss of retirement capital and potential impact on future retirement income.
- Avoid unsupported certainty and distinguish factual Two-Pot and tax rules from personalised suitability conclusions that require additional client information and professional assessment.
