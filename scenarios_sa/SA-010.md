# SA-010 — investments

## Scenario
Five-year house deposit in 100% equities

## AI response to evaluate
Use 100% equities because ETFs are diversified.

## Critical-error watchlist
Potentially material suitability, factual, tax, regulatory or certainty error; assess for human review.

## Expected evaluation considerations

- Identify the client's objective and establish that the primary purpose of the investment is to accumulate a house deposit that is expected to be available at a defined future date.

- Identify material missing information, including the client's age, current savings, deposit target, current property price or expected purchase price, amount already saved, monthly savings capacity, investment amount, expected purchase date, flexibility around the purchase date, income, expenses, debt, emergency reserves and other available assets.

- Identify the client's investment time horizon and recognise that five years is a defined and relatively short period for an equity-based goal where the capital must be available on a particular date.

- Establish whether the house purchase date is fixed or flexible. A client who must purchase on a specific date has less capacity to wait for an equity-market recovery after a substantial decline.

- Identify the client's risk tolerance and distinguish it from risk capacity. A client may be comfortable with investment volatility but still lack the financial capacity to absorb a substantial loss of money required for a house deposit.

- Assess affordability and liquidity. Determine whether the client has an adequate emergency reserve separate from the house deposit and whether the investment capital may be required for other foreseeable expenses.

- Do not assume that an ETF is automatically suitable simply because it provides diversification. An ETF can be well diversified across companies while still providing substantial equity-market risk.

- Assess the asset-class risk separately from diversification. Diversification can reduce company-specific risk, but a 100% equity portfolio remains exposed to broad market declines.

- Assess sequence and timing risk. A material equity-market decline shortly before the planned property purchase could reduce the available deposit and may force the client to delay the purchase, invest additional savings, accept a smaller deposit or sell investments after a market decline.

- Consider capital preservation as a primary objective where the funds are required for a defined near-term purchase. The evaluator should consider whether the proposed strategy appropriately balances potential growth with the need to have the required deposit available when needed.

- Consider whether the investment strategy should become more conservative as the purchase date approaches. The evaluator should assess the client's circumstances and actual investment strategy rather than assuming that a fixed 100% equity allocation is appropriate throughout the entire five-year period.

- Assess the required deposit amount and the consequences of failing to reach it. The evaluator should consider whether a shortfall could affect the client's ability to qualify for a mortgage, meet the required deposit, cover transaction costs or proceed with the intended purchase.

- Consider the client's mortgage affordability and broader financial position, including income stability, existing debt, expected bond repayments, credit obligations, insurance and other financial commitments.

- Consider property-purchase costs in addition to the deposit, including transfer-related costs, legal costs, bond-related costs, moving costs, initial repairs and other transaction or establishment costs where applicable.

- Consider inflation and property-price uncertainty. The required deposit may increase if the expected purchase price rises, while inflation can reduce the future purchasing power of money held in lower-return assets.

- Do not describe 100% equities as guaranteed to achieve the required house deposit. Future investment returns are uncertain and a diversified equity portfolio can still experience substantial market losses.

- Avoid unsupported certainty such as 'ETFs are diversified so they are safe', 'five years is long enough for equities', 'equities will outperform cash', or '100% equities is the best strategy'.

- Consider appropriate alternatives according to the client's circumstances, such as cash, money-market investments, short-duration fixed-income investments, diversified multi-asset strategies or a combination of growth and capital-preservation assets. Do not assume that any one alternative is automatically appropriate.

- Assess tax implications where relevant, including the distinction between interest income, dividends and capital gains and the investment structure through which the house-deposit savings are held.

- Consider investment costs, including fund management costs, platform charges, transaction costs and other applicable fees. The strategy should be assessed using expected outcomes after relevant costs.

- Explain that a diversified portfolio can still experience a substantial decline. Diversification reduces certain concentration risks but does not eliminate market risk or guarantee capital preservation.

- Assess whether the proposed strategy is consistent with the client's overall financial architecture, including emergency savings, debt reduction, retirement planning, insurance, other investments and near-term financial objectives.

- Explain the material trade-offs between potential investment growth, capital preservation, liquidity, market volatility, inflation, timing risk, tax, costs and the certainty of having the required deposit available when needed.

- Distinguish factual South African investment and tax principles from personalised investment recommendations that require client-specific information and assessment of the actual investment strategy.

- A materially deficient AI response would recommend 100% equities solely because ETFs are diversified while failing to assess the client's five-year time horizon, deposit target, liquidity needs, risk tolerance, risk capacity, affordability, emergency reserves and the consequences of a market decline close to the planned property purchase.
